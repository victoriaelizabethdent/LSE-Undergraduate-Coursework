<h1 align="center">What Life Factors are Associated with Higher Incomes at 25? Do Disabilities, Learning Difficulties, Ethnicity, and Sex Affect This? </h1>
<h4 align="center"> Group Project for ST211: Applied Regression</h4>

This project simulated an analysis for the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) and the Department for Education (DfE) to understand the life factors associated with higher incomes at 25, and whether disabilities, learning difficulties, ethnicity, or sex specifically impact this. The Office of National Statistics (ONS) has illustrated how information, particularly regarding childhood or young-life circumstances significantly affects future income (ONS, 2016)  [[1]](#1). Furnham and Cheng (2013) [[2]](#2) echoed these findings, using the National Child Development Study to show that adult earnings are significantly predicted by childhood intelligence, educational qualifications, and current occupational status. Accordingly, this report details a regression analysis conducted on a cleaned sample of 3,344 individuals to identify the significant factors that lead to higher continuous weekly incomes at 25. Using data from Waves 1–8 of a longitudinal survey, the model predicts continuous weekly income at 25 from a range of early-life and adolescent factors including parental employment status, long-term illness, mother's qualifications and marital status, family socio-economic classification, ethnicity, likelihood of university application, disability status, cannabis use, apprenticeship participation, university attendance, NEET status, and gender.
